CCA — Capital Cost Allowance (Depreciation)

Where to find it
FederalT2125 Schedule 8 — Capital Cost Allowance
QuébecTP-80, Schedule B — Déduction pour amortissement (DPA)
NoteFor items over ~$500 used for your business (e.g. Hyperice recovery device, laptop, camera)
How it works
You can't deduct the full cost in year 1 for capital items. Instead, you claim a % each year based on the asset class:
Class 8 (20%/yr) — most equipment, tools, fitness devices
Class 50 (55%/yr) — computers, tablets
In year 1, only 50% of the rate applies (half-year rule)
Hyperice example
$600 Hyperice, Class 8 (20%), used 100% for recovery between games:
Year 1: $600 × 20% × 50% (half-year) = $60 CCA deduction
Year 2: ($600 − $60) × 20% = $108 deduction
Enter the calculated CCA amount for this tax year here.

$60 CCA at 40%+QPP rate → saves ~$32 in tax+QPP this year

RRSP — How the math works

Where to find it
FederalRRSP contribution receipt from your financial institution (T4RSP for withdrawals)
QuébecNo separate QC slip — same deduction applies on TP-1 line 214
NoteYour RRSP room is shown on your CRA Notice of Assessment or My CRA Account
2025 limit
18% of your 2024 earned income, max $32,490. Check your Notice of Assessment for your exact room.
The math (deduction, not credit)
RRSP reduces income before tax is calculated:
Taxable income = Total income − RRSP
So $8,000 RRSP does NOT save you $8,000 in tax. It saves you $8,000 × your marginal rate.
Example at 43% marginal: $8,000 × 43% = ~$3,440 actual tax saved.
What the calculator shows
The income composition shows "−$8,000" meaning $8,000 is removed from taxable income. The tax reduction is reflected in your final federal + QC balances.
Deadline
March 3, 2025 to apply to the 2024 tax year.
Max $32,490 for 2025. Check My CRA Account for your room.

FHSA — How the math works

Where to find it
FederalFHSA contribution receipt from your bank/broker (RC3 slip)
QuébecNo separate QC slip — same deduction on TP-1 line 214B
NoteTrack your FHSA room in My CRA Account under 'FHSA'
Limits
$8,000/year, lifetime max $40,000. Unused room carries forward (up to $8,000 per year).
The math (same as RRSP deduction)
Taxable income = Total income − FHSA contribution
$8,000 FHSA saves you $8,000 × your marginal rate in tax — NOT the full $8,000.
Example at 43%: ~$3,440 tax saved today, PLUS the $8,000 grows tax-free.
Why it's better than RRSP
RRSP withdrawal is taxed later. FHSA withdrawal for a first home is completely tax-free. You get the deduction now AND pay zero tax on the money when you use it to buy.
Eligibility
Must be a first-time buyer, Canadian resident. Can hold up to 15 years. If unused, transfer to RRSP with no tax impact.
Max $8,000/yr. Deductible AND tax-free withdrawal — extremely powerful!
